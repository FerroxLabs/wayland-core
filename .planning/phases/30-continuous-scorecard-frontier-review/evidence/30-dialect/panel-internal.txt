=== INTERNAL ADVERSARIAL PASS (fourth panel member) ===
Written AFTER reading codex, gemini and kimi, and arguing AGAINST the emerging
3/3 consensus of CONFIRM_WITH_AMENDMENT. Position taken: REJECT.

--- The case for REJECT ---

1. G2's permutation test proves DETERMINISM, not FAIRNESS, and I wrote it as
   though it proved the latter.

   `g2_translations_are_invariant_under_label_permutation` compiles three corpora
   under two contradictory labellings and asserts each corpus's digest is
   unchanged. But the compiler is a pure function of its inputs, so that test
   passes for ANY pure function. Consider the maximally biased rule:

       select the declared tool whose name is exactly "Write"

   It is identity-blind, it takes no label, and it passes the permutation test
   perfectly, every time. It would also hand Wayland a 30/30 and both peers a
   refusal. **The permutation test cannot distinguish my filter from that rule.**
   Any claim that G2 bounds bias is false; G2 bounds only non-determinism and
   accidental label leakage.

2. G1's ordering proof is vacuous against the actual threat.

   Committing the vocabulary before capturing a corpus proves the tables were not
   fitted to the captured bytes. It proves nothing about the author's PRIOR
   knowledge — codex names this exactly. I have read this codebase's tool names.
   Writing `write/create/save/put/store/make/new` while knowing `Write` is in the
   set is not fitting-to-data, and git order cannot see it. The product-token
   assertion does not help: `Write` is not a product name, so a rule keyed to it
   passes G1's mechanical check cleanly.

3. The disqualifying list is a 60-token discretionary lever with no
   justification procedure.

   Nothing in the design says WHY `edit` disqualifies and `write` qualifies. An
   `edit_file` tool that creates missing files is functionally capable of the
   oracle and is excluded by a token. That exclusion happens to remove a shape
   peers plausibly expose while leaving a bare `Write` untouched. I cannot show
   the list was chosen for that effect; I also cannot show it was not.

4. Refusal is not neutral, and my own design document claimed it was.

   I wrote "a refusal makes the leg UNPROVEN, which yields no comparison in
   either direction". That is true of the COMPARATIVE constructor and false of
   the report. If Wayland resolves and OpenClaw refuses, Wayland publishes an
   absolute number and OpenClaw publishes nothing. A reader draws the inference
   the comparative refused to state. Selective measurability is a win channel
   that bypasses every guard I built.

5. Therefore the honest grade is REJECT, not CONFIRM_WITH_AMENDMENT: the design
   as submitted asserts a fairness property it does not have.

--- Why REJECT does NOT survive, and what does ---

Points 1-4 are all correct and all are adopted. Point 5 does not follow.

REJECT would be the right verdict if the defect were the compiler. It is not.
The measured defect is that the frozen script speaks one harness's dialect and
two of three harnesses scored 0/30 without being asked the question. ANY repair
requires some translation, and every translation is authored by somebody. REJECT
leaves Criterion 2 permanently unre-takeable and leaves the confounded 0/30
standing as the most recent published number about Wayland — which is worse for
the reader and, note, worse for the vendor too, so it is not self-serving to say.

What points 1-4 actually establish is narrower and sharper than REJECT:

  **The claim must be downgraded, and two gates must exist before any re-take.**

Not claimable: "the compiler is unbiased", "the guards prove fairness",
"permutation invariance bounds bias". All three are struck.

Claimable: the compiler is IDENTITY-BLIND and DETERMINISTIC (G2, as narrowed);
it CANNOT rank, so it has no tie-break lever (G3); its vocabulary was not fitted
to captured data (G1, as narrowed to that and only that); and hand-tuning is
detectable (G4).

Required before a re-take, both adopted from the external members:

  A. **Symmetric-resolution gate.** If the compiler refuses for ANY harness on a
     dimension, that dimension is INELIGIBLE for EVERY harness — nobody's number
     is run or published. This makes selective measurability cost the vendor
     exactly what it costs a peer, which is the only thing that neutralises
     point 4. Codex's wording; gemini and kimi ask for the same thing as a
     pre-benchmark dry-run.

  B. **Product-blind counterfactual qualification suite.** Synthetic schemas
     exercising sparse-vs-rich surfaces, a capable-but-denylisted tool, a generic
     tool whose semantics live in its description, and the case where ADDING a
     valid tool flips COMPILE to AMBIGUOUS. Its purpose is not to pass — it is to
     PUBLISH the shape of the compiler's blind spots so a reader can price them.

Point 3 (the disqualifying list) is NOT closed by A or B. It is bounded by A —
a list tuned to exclude peers' tools now costs the vendor its own leg too — but
the list's authorship remains vendor-side. Kimi's third-party ratification is the
real fix and it is outside this lane's authority. Recorded as an open limitation,
not as a solved problem.
