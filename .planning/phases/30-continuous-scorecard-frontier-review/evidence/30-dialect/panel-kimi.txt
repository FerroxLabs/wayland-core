• ## Q1 — Where the win is still manufactured

  **(a) The disqualifying list is the lever, and G1 does not guard it.** G1 proves the vocabulary wasn't tuned to the *captured corpus*. It cannot prove it wasn't tuned to knowledge the author already had — and the author *is Wayland*. Wayland's own tool surface is known to the designer with certainty before any capture happens. So the design process that "proves" neutrality is: author a ~60-token disqualifying list, mentally run it against Wayland's `Write`/`Read`/`Edit`/`Bash` surface (passes, trivially), and never need to run it against Hermes's or OpenClaw's surface — because the AMBIGUOUS/NO_CANDIDATE refusal rules mean peer-side failures cost the *peers* a leg, not the vendor. The asymmetry isn't in the filter; it's in who bears the cost of the filter's failures. A 60-token exclusion list against a 7-token action list is 60 independent chances to kill a peer candidate and 7 chances to find one. Nothing in G1–G5 requires the author to demonstrate the list against any schema but its own before freezing it. The permutation test (G2) proves the compiler is blind to labels *at compile time*; it says nothing about whether the tables were blind to identity *at authoring time*. They could not have been — the author's own product was sitting in front of them.

  **(b) AMBIGUOUS refusal converts tool richness into measurability loss, asymmetrically.** Wayland's surface, per the v1 result, is sparse and single-purpose: one `Write`, one `Read`. It will essentially never produce two survivors. A peer exposing both `write_file` and `create_file` — or `write_file` and `save_note` — hits two survivors and gets *refused, not measured*. The protocol thereby measures a property correlated with having a minimal tool surface and reports richer harnesses as UNPROVEN. "Exposes more tools" literally becomes "produces no number." And since v1 already showed Hermes 30/30 on the canonical script, the pipeline that would have produced a peer-favouring number is exactly the one most likely to be refused under v2. That is not a neutral cost; it is a cost schedule that falls on the harnesses that won v1.

  **(c) Excluding descriptions helps Wayland.** The stated reason (prose advantages verbose vendors) is plausible, but look at who has clean dictionary-token names: the vendor. `Write`, `Read` tokenize perfectly. A peer whose tool is named, say, `fs_put` or `str_replace`-family-but-not-on-the-list, or anything idiosyncratic, gets NO_CANDIDATE — and descriptions were the one channel that could have rescued a legitimately-write-capable tool with a non-dictionary name. Dropping descriptions is "neutral" only if all three harnesses draw names from the same vocabulary the author pre-registered. The author knows one harness that does.

  ## Q2 — Wire capture

  The capture mechanism itself is genuinely neutral — the `tools` array is emitted by the harness, and digesting it (G4) is sound. What is not neutral is the *wire*. The fixture is the vendor's loopback server: it authors the tool-result payloads, the injected failures for the recovery legs, the error phrasing, the canary bytes. Recovery is a scored metric, and recovery is parsed from fixture-authored text. A harness whose retry logic was developed against OpenAI-style error envelopes (the vendor's, presumably) reads fixture errors natively; a peer reads whatever the vendor's fixture decided an error looks like. There is no G-guard on fixture response phrasing at all. Also: participation requires the peer to be configurable onto an arbitrary OpenAI-compatible base_url — a selection condition that happens to describe the vendor's own integration surface. Wire capture neutralizes *what tools are declared*; it does nothing to neutralize *what the environment says back*.

  ## Q3 — Should it run?

  Not on this design. The disqualifying/action/slot tables are the scoring function wearing a filter's clothes, and they were authored by an interested party who had already seen the v1 outcome and knew with certainty how its own surface would fare against them. Freezing v2 before measurement is worthless as a guard when the *authoring* of v2 was post-hoc — this is protocol shopping with a freeze step. The minimum that must exist first: the three vocabulary tables and the disqualifying list must be fixed by a party with no stake in the result — or derived mechanically from a pre-registered external corpus of third-party agent-harness schemas selected by someone else — with a published per-harness dry-run showing survivors/refusals for all three harnesses *before* any scored leg runs, and refusal rates committed to publication as first-class results. If the dry run shows Wayland compiling and a peer refusing, the tables are wrong, not the peer.

  ## Q4 — Is refusal neutral?

  No. UNPROVEN is information-asymmetric publication. If Wayland resolves and Hermes refuses, the published artifact contains a Wayland number and a Hermes blank. Every reader — and every future benchmark citation — treats a blank as absence of capability, not as a protocol artifact. "No comparison in either direction" is technically true of the *comparative type* and practically false of the *published table*: one column has 30/30, the other has a footnote. Given that the vendor is also the publisher, refusal functions as a soft score of zero for the refused party with none of the falsifiability of a real zero. Minimum fix: refusals are headline results published in the same table with refusal reason and candidate counts, and any leg-set where refusal rates differ across harnesses invalidates the comparison entirely — reported as "benchmark failed to measure X," not "X is UNPROVEN."
