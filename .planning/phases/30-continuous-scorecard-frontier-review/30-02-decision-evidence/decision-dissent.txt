## A vote-extraction defect measured, not assumed

At 22:39 a poll loop reported codex as having voted. It had not: `codex exec` ECHOES the
prompt, and the prompt contains the literal string `PANEL_POSITION=<CONFIRM|OVERTURN|...>`,
so `grep -c 'PANEL_POSITION='` matched the question rather than an answer. Codex did not
actually return until 22:44. Extraction was tightened to
`grep -oE 'PANEL_POSITION=(CONFIRM_WITH_AMENDMENT|CONFIRM|OVERTURN)'` with `tail -1`, which
cannot match the echoed template because the next character there is `<`.

The converse trap was also measured on the same artifacts:

    grep -cE '^PANEL_POSITION=' panel-kimi.txt   ->  0     (anchored: kimi's vote is LOST)
    grep -cE  'PANEL_POSITION=' panel-kimi.txt   ->  3     (unanchored: vote present)

kimi indents and bullet-prefixes. An anchored regex would have silently turned this into a
three-way panel. Both defects are the same class as a self-passing gate and both were caught
only by reading the captured output.

## The decision

Adopt `hybrid-scripted-plus-declared-live`, amended by the panel as follows. Where members
differed, the choice and its reason are stated.

**A1 — Scope. Unanimous.** Every measurement carries a required closed-enum scope tag. The
loopback tier is `SCRIPTED_HARNESS`. No measurement taken under it may be read as model
quality, real-world task success, or dollar cost. Codex's exact framing is adopted: call it a
scripted agent-harness benchmark, never an agent-quality benchmark.

**A2 — Cognitive tax is NOT MEASURABLE in this tier. Unanimous, and this is the amendment
that costs us a dimension.** All three external members refused to proxy it. Gemini: "drop
this dimension; proxying via wall-clock measures performance, not cognitive load." Codex:
"configuration-line counts, command counts and elapsed time are not cognitive tax — encode
NOT_MEASURED, not zero." Kimi: an intervention-count proxy is real but "measures prompt
frequency, not cognitive load, and the three tools have different interaction models so the
counts are not on a common scale." Accepted in full: all three cognitive-tax legs are
UNPROVEN by construction of the protocol, before any trial runs, with the substitution point
named (a scripted human-operator protocol in the live tier). F30-03 is therefore
INCOMPLETE on one of its five dimensions and this plan says so rather than inventing a number.

**A3 — Four verdict states, not three. Kimi's amendment, adopted over the plan's own text.**
The plan specifies a closed THREE-member direction enum (ahead / behind / indistinguishable).
Kimi's objection is decisive and neither of the others contradicts it: "'CI contains zero →
tie' silently converts low power into declared equivalence." A wide interval means
INCONCLUSIVE, not indistinguishable. Codex independently reached the same four-state shape
(`A_BETTER / A_WORSE / PRACTICAL_TIE / INCONCLUSIVE`); gemini's two-state shape is the
weakest of the three on exactly this point and is not followed. The enum therefore carries
four members. Both added members are NON-DIRECTIONAL, so the plan's hard rule — refuse a
directional verdict whenever the delta interval contains zero — is preserved exactly and is
in fact strengthened: a directional verdict now additionally requires the nearer interval
endpoint to clear the tie band. Deviation from the plan's letter, recorded here and in the
summary, in the direction of refusing MORE claims rather than fewer.

**A4 — Trial counts. Members split 30 / 30 / 10.** Codex 30 per leg with the rule-of-three
justification and the explicit limitation that 30/30 successes still yields a Wilson lower
bound near 88.6%, so no "better than 95% reliability" claim is available. Kimi 30 for
proportions, 15 for continuous. Gemini 10, on the ground that a scripted fixture leaves only
scheduling jitter. Taken: **30 for proportions, 15 for continuous.** Gemini's 10 is rejected
because its own reasoning cuts the other way — if the informative event is a rare
nondeterministic failure, a small n is exactly what fails to observe it. This count is frozen
now, before feasibility is known. If a leg cannot reach its count it is UNPROVEN; the count
is never lowered to make a leg reportable.

**A5 — Intervals. Unanimous on Wilson + percentile bootstrap; codex adds one correction.**
Wilson for single proportions; **Newcombe's Wilson-based interval for a DIFFERENCE of
proportions**, not the subtraction of two Wilson endpoints, which codex flagged and the
others did not raise. Percentile bootstrap with 10,000 resamples for continuous quantities,
seeded from the protocol so a rerun reproduces the bounds. Clopper-Pearson rejected by both
members who addressed it: conservative, wider at n=30, and it does not repair an
underpowered design. Kimi's BCa suggestion is NOT taken — plain percentile is what the plan
budgets for and BCa's extra machinery is not worth the surface here; kimi's accompanying
requirement IS taken, that the raw min/max is reported alongside every continuous interval.
Codex's degenerate case is taken: identical observations emit ZERO_EMPIRICAL_VARIANCE rather
than a zero-width interval presented as precision.

**A6 — Tie band, and it is not double-counting. Codex and kimi agree; both give the same
reason.** The zero rule is a DIFFERENCE test; the tie band is an EQUIVALENCE test. They
answer different questions, which is why A3's fourth state is needed to hold the difference.
Band frozen at 0.05 absolute for proportions (both members proposed exactly this) and, for
cost, codex's ln(1.05) on the mean-ratio scale — 5% — rather than kimi's 10%, taking the
tighter of the two because a wider band declares more ties and a tie is the verdict that
costs us nothing to claim.

**A7 — Stop rule. Codex's numbers, kimi's scoring policy.** 120 s to first fixture request,
120 s inactivity, 600 s absolute; SIGTERM to the process group, 5 s, SIGKILL. A timeout is a
SCORED FAILURE and is never discarded — kimi: "excluding hangs flattens flaky tools into
looking reliable; a hang is a failure the user experiences." Three consecutive timeouts halt
that leg as FAILED_INCOMPLETE with the trials actually run recorded.

**A8 — Per-trial isolation. Kimi and codex; not in the starting position.** A fresh fixture
instance, a fresh workspace and a fresh process tree per trial, with a unique security
canary per trial. The starting position said "ONE shared loopback fixture"; both members
objected that one shared FIFO instance across trials couples them. The fixture binds port 0,
so this costs nothing. The starting position is amended.

**A9 — The 409 reclassification. This is the dissent's amendment and it is binding.** A
fixture `unexpected_request` violation is classified HARNESS_INCOMPATIBLE and is scored as
neither success nor task failure. Codex: "protocol incompatibility must be distinguished
from scored task failure." Without this, gemini's Q7 scenario — a peer that requests B then A
gets a 409 and is recorded as failing — silently converts a difference in request ORDER into
a Wayland win.

## The dissent, recorded because it is not resolved

**All four members independently named the same defect, and this plan is fenced from fixing
it.** The fixture is FIFO-cursored and is the dialect Wayland's own contract tests were built
against. Gemini's countermeasure is a content-routing fixture; kimi's is a corpus derived
from the peers' own recorded traffic plus external sign-off; codex's is an
independently-controlled task corpus with peer-approved adapters. Every one of those requires
editing the shared meter or authoring a peer-derived corpus, and
`crates/wcore-eval-scenarios/src/fixtures/openai.rs` is a hard scope fence in this plan,
gate-checked untouched.

So the home-field bias is REAL, NAMED BY EVERY MEMBER, and OPEN. A9 bounds it — it can no
longer be banked as a peer failure — but it does not remove it. This is carried as a seam
request rather than papered over, and any 30-03 claim resting on a cross-tool comparison
inherits it, exactly as 30-01's finding on `PEER-PROBE-2026-07-26` propagates.

**The internal pass's second objection is unanswered by any type and becomes a binding
requirement on task 3.** `ComparativeResultV1` prevents a false comparative NUMBER for an
unrun peer; it does nothing about the impression left by a one-sided results PAGE. Task 3
must therefore lead with the leg accounting before any number, must state in plain words that
an UNPROVEN peer leg is not a Wayland win and yields no comparison at all, and must quote
every provisioning failure verbatim so a reader can see a peer failed to BUILD rather than
failed to PERFORM.
