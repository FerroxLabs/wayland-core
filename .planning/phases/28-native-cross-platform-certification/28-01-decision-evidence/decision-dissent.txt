DISSENT — Phase 28 acceptance gate. The losing arguments in their own terms.

The external panel was unanimous (3-0) and the internal pass concurred conditionally, making it
4-0. A unanimous panel is the one most likely to have agreed too fast, so recording the losing
arguments is MORE important here, not less. This is what a later reader needs in order to
reopen the decision if the risk in it lands.

=== ARGUMENT FOR c4-literal, in its own terms, and it is the morally strongest option ===

Criterion 4 says what it says. "Zero findings remain at every severity before acceptance" is
not ambiguous prose that happens to admit a weaker reading; it is a distinctive claim, and it is
distinctive precisely BECAUSE it exceeds the ordinary rule. A certification is not a feature
phase. It is the artifact a release stands on, and it is consumed by people who were not in the
room. The only certification claim that survives contact with a hostile reader is one that
needs no footnote: this candidate had no known open defect of any kind, at any severity, on any
platform. Every weaker claim is a negotiation, and every negotiation is conducted by the party
with the deadline.

The counter-argument that carried is narrower than the 4-0 vote makes it look, and it rests on
exactly one fact rather than on the option's merits: Criterion 4 was authored on 2026-07-19,
six days BEFORE the severity amendment of 2026-07-25. Had those dates been reversed — had the
criterion been written by someone who already knew the standing rule and chose to exceed it for
the certification gate specifically — this dissent would be the decision, and c4-disposition
would be an agent overruling an informed human instruction. Anyone reopening this should check
that date claim first, because it is the whole load-bearing structure. If the human ever
restates Criterion 4 in its literal form KNOWING the current severity policy, that restatement
supersedes this decision immediately and without further panel.

Its defeat, stated plainly: the literal rule is the one this program ran and measured, and it
produced 74 plans over two weeks with the phase no closer to done. Worse, it is unreachable
here — bash under Windows AppContainer cannot be made to work at any budget, so an empty-ledger
gate could only ever be reached by relabelling that finding out of existence. A rule that can
only be satisfied by dishonesty produces dishonesty.

=== ARGUMENT FOR c4-standing, in its own terms ===

It is the only option with no new machinery, no new rubric, no new cross-audit obligation and no
new receipt fields. Every other phase in this program runs on RULE B. Making the certification
phase run on a bespoke variant means the phase most in need of predictability is the one
operating under a rule invented for it, by the agent that then has to satisfy it. There is a
real, unglamorous argument that consistency across thirty phases is worth more than a locally
better rule in one of them.

Its defeat: it does the WORSE narrowing. Dropping MEDIUM and LOW from the gate entirely means a
MEDIUM finding need not even be RECORDED, which directly defeats the "no critical case is
skipped" half of F28-04 and makes sub-HIGH findings invisible at the exact moment visibility is
the entire product. c4-disposition costs paperwork; c4-standing costs the ledger.

=== ARGUMENT FOR c4-escalate, in its own terms ===

The conflict is between two documents the human owns, written by him, six days apart. He is the
only party who can say which one he meant. An agent choosing between two of a human's own
instructions — and choosing the one that happens to be easier to satisfy — is not obviously
better than asking, however well the choice is reasoned and however much the reasoning is
written down.

Its defeat: the program's operating rule reserves to the human only irreversible outward actions
and inputs no agent can produce, and names them exhaustively — merge, PR, tag, release, issue
closure, evidence-ref deletion, credentials. Evidence-acceptance policy is explicitly not among
them, and the measured cost of the opposite habit was 18 blocking human gates across 32 plans,
the single largest contributor to a two-week wall clock. The decision is also fully reversible:
it is written down, its dissent is written down, and a one-line instruction from Sean overturns
it.

=== THE OBJECTION THAT NEARLY CHANGED THE OUTCOME ===

Recorded because it says something about the PANEL rather than about the options.

c4-disposition is c4-standing with paperwork. Strip the machinery and the candidate is
byte-identical under both, the defects are identical under both, and only the prose differs. A
receipt is not a control: it does not stop a defect reaching a user, it records that someone
knew. All three external members treated "enumerated in the receipt" as though it were a
mitigation, and all three did so in the same direction — which is one argument counted three
times, not three independent confirmations.

That objection was not defeated by the vote. It was defeated by attaching amendment A2, which
removes the findings the objection actually cares about from the accept path entirely: anything
contradicting one of Phase 28's four Success Criteria is CRITICAL or HIGH by construction and
can only be FIXED or DISPROVED. Two findings this program is carrying TODAY at sub-HIGH move
across that line immediately — the descendant-process-tree reap failure (Criterion 2 says "no
orphan process") and the AppContainer silent-sandbox-disable (Criterion 1 is sandbox probes).

The consequence for a later reader: A2 IS THE DECISION. If a future plan, executor or reviewer
keeps c4-disposition's four dispositions but drops or softens A2 — by scoring findings at their
inherited severity, or by letting a criterion-contradicting finding take the ACCEPT path — then
this decision has silently become c4-standing with paperwork, the dissent above becomes correct,
and the certification is worth materially less than its own text claims. Check A2 before
trusting any Phase 28 acceptance.
