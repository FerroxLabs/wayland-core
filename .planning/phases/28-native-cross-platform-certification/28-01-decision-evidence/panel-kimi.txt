**The crux first: is "dispositioned" an honest reading of "resolved", or scope reduction in
reconciliation's clothes?**

The answer turns on what Criterion 4 is actually protecting, and its own text answers that.
F28-04 does not say "every finding is fixed." It says "no critical case is skipped and every
finding at every severity is resolved." The two clauses are a pair: the first is about
**coverage** (nothing skipped), the second is about **accounting** (nothing dropped). The
protective core of the criterion is that the finding ledger is complete and every entry has a
closed fate — not that every entry's fate is "fixed." A program that fixes nothing but silently
deletes MEDIUM findings from the ledger satisfies the literal c4-literal gate while violating
the criterion's actual purpose. A program that fixes CRITICAL/HIGH and carries three MEDIUMs as
named, owned, receipt-enumerated deferrals satisfies the purpose while violating the dictionary
sense of "resolved." Honest reconciliation reads the criterion by its internal structure; scope
reduction reads it by whatever the executor finds convenient. c4-disposition is the former,
*provided* the anti-laundering machinery (written severity rubric, independent cross-audit for
any CRITICAL/HIGH downgrade, receipt enumeration) is real and not decorative. Without that
machinery it would be scope reduction — which is precisely why the option carries it.

**The measured evidence.** This is not a theoretical tradeoff; the program ran both rules. The
literal rule (block on LOW) produced 74 plans in two weeks. The amended rule (RULE B) produced
3 of 3 Success Criteria in 4 plans. That is the single strongest data point in the decision, and
c4-literal asks the program to repeal its one measured process improvement — on the phase with
the largest matrix and the most surface for findings, where the loop cost would be worst.
Worse: several open sub-HIGH items are architectural (bash under Windows AppContainer cannot
exist by construction). Under a literal "fixed" gate those items are unfixable at any budget, so
the gate can only be met by reclassifying them — forcing the severity-laundering attack that
c4-disposition merely has to defend against. c4-literal is not just slow; it is unreachable as
written and corruptible in practice.

**The certification argument cuts toward rigor, but toward rigor of a specific kind.** A signed
receipt that a release will rely on is a stronger claim than a feature phase makes — but the
strength the receipt needs is **honesty and visibility**, not an empty ledger. The program's
measured catastrophic failure mode is not accumulated MEDIUMs; it is evidence that proves
nothing (177 unfailable gates, 283 tests with no execution evidence). A receipt that says "zero
open defects" while an AppContainer finding was quietly relabeled is exactly that failure mode
with a signature on it. A receipt that enumerates "three accepted MEDIUMs: Windows descendant
reap (owner X, BACKLOG-412, rationale: ...), ..." is a stronger certification artifact than the
fiction of zero — Phase 29 and the release can actually rely on it, because what it says is
true.

**Why not the other two.** c4-standing commits the sin it accuses c4-disposition of: it narrows
a written Success Criterion to fit the intended evidence, and it does the *worse* narrowing —
dropping MEDIUM/LOW from the gate entirely means a MEDIUM finding need not even be recorded,
which directly defeats "no critical case is skipped." c4-disposition keeps every finding in the
ledger and in the receipt; c4-standing makes sub-HIGH findings invisible at the exact moment
visibility matters most. c4-escalate fails the program's explicit operating rule:
evidence-acceptance policy is reserved to the panel, not the human, and the measured cost of
routing decisions upward (18 blocking gates, the largest contributor to the two-week wall
clock) is documented in the same record as the 74 plans. The conflict is between two documents,
but the program's own precedence rules (decide well, don't stop; never redefine success
downward; don't reintroduce the measured failure mode) resolve it without an appeal.

**Gameability of the chosen option.** Two attacks: severity laundering (downgrade HIGH to
MEDIUM to buy acceptance) and accept-everything (a lazy executor accepts a genuinely serious
MEDIUM). The rubric plus mandatory independent cross-audit on any CRITICAL/HIGH downgrade
addresses the first; the second is mitigated but not eliminated — a named rationale, owner,
BACKLOG id, and receipt enumeration raise the cost of a bad accept from invisible to signed,
which is the same accountability mechanism the program already trusts for its PARTIAL phase
grades. No option is ungameable; c4-disposition is the only one whose failure mode is *visible
in the signed artifact* rather than hidden behind it.

The certification phase's receipt should certify the truth with full accounting, not a number
that was reached by the cheapest available reclassification.
