=== PHASE 28 ACCEPTANCE-GATE DECISION: how must "zero findings remain at every severity" be
    implemented as a certification acceptance gate, given a standing program rule that says the
    opposite for MEDIUM and below? ===

You are one of four independent reviewers. Answer from the evidence below.
End your response with EXACTLY one line: PANEL_POSITION=<option-id>
where <option-id> is one of: c4-disposition | c4-literal | c4-standing | c4-escalate

--- CONTEXT ---

wayland-core is a Rust multi-provider AI agent CLI. Its delivery program ("phases 20-30") is
about to plan Phase 28, "Native Cross-Platform Certification". Phase 28 does not build product
features. It certifies an exact candidate build produced by phases 24-27 against a hostile
native platform matrix (macOS, Linux, Windows), a 1,000-session and concurrent-child soak, and
signed evidence receipts.

TWO WRITTEN RULES COLLIDE, and the collision decides how the phase's acceptance gate is built.

RULE A -- Phase 28's own text, from the program ROADMAP and REQUIREMENTS, verbatim:

  ROADMAP Phase 28 Success Criterion 4:
    "Zero findings remain at every severity before acceptance."

  REQUIREMENTS F28-04:
    "No critical case is skipped and every finding at every severity is resolved before
     certification acceptance."

RULE B -- the program's standing severity policy, in AGENTS.md and in the lane operating brief,
verbatim:

  "Severity policy: CRITICAL/HIGH must be fixed, or disproved with executable evidence.
   MEDIUM and below go to BACKLOG, non-blocking. Do not invent a stricter rule -- that is what
   turned Phase 20 into a 74-plan loop lasting two weeks."

--- MEASURED HISTORY THAT BEARS ON THIS, not opinion ---

1. Phase 20 of this same program ran under a stricter rule in which a LOW finding blocked
   advancement (the tool config literally had security_block_on=low). Phase 20 metastasised to
   74 plans over roughly two weeks. The rule was then amended -- commit d0837aa7 -- to exactly
   RULE B, and the config changed to security_block_on=high. The very next phase, 20A, which
   ran under the amended rule and a hard four-plan cap, closed all three of its Success Criteria
   in 4 plans. That is the strongest single piece of evidence in this decision, and it points
   away from the literal reading.

2. Phase 20A nevertheless closed with FOUR requirements deliberately OPEN (r2, r8, r12, r13),
   each with a written disposition, and the phase was still called complete because none of the
   four gated a Success Criterion. So this program already has a working precedent for
   "recorded open item, explicitly dispositioned, does not block acceptance". It has NO
   precedent for "every item at every severity actually fixed".

3. This program's most expensive recurring failure is NOT unfixed low-severity defects. It is
   evidence that proves nothing: a plan-gate linter run over five phase plan sets that had
   already passed three rounds of adversarial human review found 177 gates that could not
   possibly fail. A separate audit found 283 tests with no execution evidence and roughly 145
   that ran in no workflow at all. The program's demonstrated risk is silent absence, not
   accumulated MEDIUMs.

4. Counter-evidence that cuts the other way, and it is real: this is a CERTIFICATION phase whose
   output is a signed receipt that a downstream phase (29, supply chain and release integrity)
   and ultimately a release will rely on. Phases 24-27 all graded themselves honestly and all
   are currently PARTIAL. Several already-known defects on this candidate are classified
   below HIGH and are carried as "known-red, non-gating", including a Windows descendant
   process-tree reap failure, two Windows DACL unit tests, and a worker output-exhaustion test
   that exceeds its time budget. A certification that signs a receipt while a process-reap
   defect stays open is signing something.

--- OPTIONS. Do not re-scope them. ---

c4-disposition:
  "Resolved" is implemented as DISPOSITIONED, not as FIXED. The acceptance gate is: zero
  findings at any severity lack an explicit, recorded, evidence-backed disposition. CRITICAL and
  HIGH must be FIXED or DISPROVED WITH EXECUTABLE EVIDENCE -- accept is not available at those
  severities. MEDIUM and LOW may additionally be ACCEPTED or DEFERRED, each requiring a named
  rationale, a named owner, and a BACKLOG id, and each is enumerated inside the signed receipt
  so it is visible at acceptance and at release rather than absorbed. Because the obvious attack
  on this option is severity laundering -- downgrading a HIGH to MEDIUM to buy the weaker
  disposition -- severity is assigned by a written rubric, and any downgrade from an initially
  assessed CRITICAL/HIGH requires an independent cross-audit and is recorded in the receipt.
  PROS: preserves what Criterion 4 is actually protecting, which is that nothing is silently
  dropped and no critical case is skipped -- the clause it is paired with in F28-04 is about
  coverage and accounting; keeps RULE B intact so the 74-plan failure mode is not reintroduced;
  makes every accepted defect visible in the artifact the release depends on.
  CONS: "resolved" is being read as "dispositioned", which is a weaker word than the criterion
  chose; a determined executor can still accept a genuinely serious MEDIUM; it adds rubric and
  cross-audit machinery to the phase.

c4-literal:
  Criterion 4 means what it says. Every finding at every severity, LOW included, is fixed before
  the certification is accepted. Phase 28 does not close until the finding ledger is empty.
  PROS: strongest possible certification claim; no judgement call about severity can weaken it;
  a signed receipt then means the candidate had no known open defect of any kind.
  CONS: it is precisely the rule that produced the 74-plan two-week loop on this program, and
  the amendment away from it is the single measured process improvement the program has; several
  currently-open sub-HIGH items are architectural (bash cannot run under Windows AppContainer at
  all, by construction) and cannot be "fixed" at any budget, so the gate would be unreachable
  and would be met by reclassifying items rather than by fixing them -- the same laundering
  attack, but forced rather than optional.

c4-standing:
  RULE B governs unchanged. Phase 28's acceptance gate is CRITICAL/HIGH fixed-or-disproved;
  MEDIUM and below go to BACKLOG and do not appear in the gate. Criterion 4 is recorded as
  narrowed to that reading.
  PROS: simplest, fully consistent with the rest of the program, zero new machinery.
  CONS: it narrows a written Success Criterion to fit the evidence the phase intends to produce.
  This program has an explicit standing prohibition on exactly that move -- "never redefine
  success downward when you hit a wall" -- and a prior agent on this program invented a fake
  termination state to dodge an artifact it wrongly believed unobtainable. Narrowing also
  discards the criterion's real content: "no critical case is SKIPPED" is about coverage, and
  dropping MEDIUM/LOW from the gate entirely means a MEDIUM finding need not even be recorded.

c4-escalate:
  Do not decide. Route the conflict to the human owner and block Phase 28 planning on his ruling.
  PROS: the conflict is between two documents the human owns, so he is the correct authority.
  CONS: the program's own operating rule says a decision point is an instruction to decide well,
  not to stop, and reserves to the human only irreversible outward actions -- merging, tagging,
  releasing, closing issues, credentials -- explicitly NOT evidence-acceptance policy. Measured
  cost of the opposite habit: 18 blocking human gates across 32 plans was the single largest
  contributor to Phase 20's two-week wall clock.
