### 1. The Quality Proxy
First, I am validating your quality proxy. Measuring invariant adherence against a warm-up window is NOT a semantic "quality" test—it is a determinism and stability test. However, it is the correct choice here. Injecting live LLM calls introduces network jitter and third-party model variance, which would make mechanical gating impossible. Comparing a surface against its own warm-up behavior on the same host is the only way to isolate the binary's integrity from the ecosystem. Quality and performance must be banded separately, as one is a binomial proportion (pass/fail) and the other is a continuous variable (time).

### 2. Window Definition and Statistics
- **Early Window:** Sessions 101 to 300. (Sessions 1-100 are discarded from baseline to eliminate cold-start OS paging, JIT, and filesystem cache warming, which artificially inflate early latency and mask later degradation).
- **Late Window:** Sessions 801 to 1000.
- **Statistic for Performance:** **p95 wall-clock latency**. p50 is too blind to tail degradation (where resource exhaustion typically manifests). p100 (Max) is hostage to Hetzner's noisy neighbor CPU spikes. p95 mathematically shears off the top 5% of host-induced outlier spikes while retaining visibility into systemic algorithmic degradation.
- **Statistic for Quality:** **Invariant failure rate** (failed sessions / total sessions in the window). 

### 3. The Numeric Bands (The Mechanical Gate)
Since we have no historical baseline, the absolute floors must be indisputable sanity checks, and the drift bands must be wide enough to absorb ambient host noise but tight enough to catch O(N) resource exhaustion.

**Performance (Latency) Bands:**
*   **Absolute Floor:** Early Window p50 latency MUST BE `< 1.5 seconds`. (Reasoning: It is a local CLI. **Quality (Determinism) Bands:**
*   **Absolute Floor:** Total run invariant failure rate MUST BE `< 2%`. (Reasoning: Solves the "uniformly bad from session 1" problem. If the binary fails its own invariants >2% of the time, the build is unstable).
