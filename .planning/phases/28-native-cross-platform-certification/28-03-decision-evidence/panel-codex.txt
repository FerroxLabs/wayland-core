1. **Choose C: drift primary plus loose absolute sanity ceilings.** A misses a binary that is broken or uniformly slow from invocation one. B pretends unsupported hardware-specific numbers are empirical. D is circular and cannot certify anything. Each OS must pass independently; results are never pooled.

2. **Windows and statistics.** Commit the 116-surface roster, hash, and deterministic cycle schedule before execution. Across the 1,000 invocations:

   - Occurrence 1 of each surface: warm-up.
   - Occurrences 2–4: early window, 348 sessions.
   - Occurrence 5: guard interval.
   - Occurrences 6–8: late window, 348 sessions.
   - Remaining 72 ninth occurrences: excluded from drift, included in absolute gates.

   For each surface, calculate the median of its three early latencies and its three late latencies. Define:

   `drift_s = (late_median_s - early_median_s) / max(early_median_s, 0.100 seconds)`

   Sort the 116 surface drifts. Use nearest-rank p50 (item 58) and p95 (item 111). Medians suppress one transient spike among three observations; surface-level p50 detects broad degradation, while p95 prevents regressions concentrated in several commands. A mean would be too hostage to shared-host outliers and could obscure which surfaces deteriorated.

3. **Committed numbers.** Performance passes only when all are true on every OS:

   - Surface-drift p50 ≤ 15%.
   - Surface-drift p95 ≤ 35%.
   - All-session wall-clock p95 ≤ 10 seconds.
   - No invocation exceeds 60 seconds; 60 seconds is also the harness timeout.

   The 100 ms denominator floor prevents meaningless percentage explosions for millisecond commands. The absolute ceilings are intentionally loose but not decorative: an offline, supposedly short-lived CLI taking over 10 seconds routinely—or over one minute once—is operationally defective regardless of hardware. These numbers are not empirically “discovered”; they are defensible only as prospective product limits whose consequences the project accepts before observing results. Using the same limits on every platform is more honest than fabricating platform-specific baselines.

4. **Quality is gated separately from performance.** It is not averaged into a composite score and does not receive a latency-style percentage allowance:

   - Zero unexpected exits, signals, panics, or timeouts across all 1,000 sessions.
   - Zero exit-class or output-invariant mismatches after warm-up.
   - Every warm-up result must satisfy a committed invariant constructor and generic sanity schema.

   Warm-up may bind variable values, but it may not define “whatever happened” as correct. Required fields, allowed exit classes, parseability, and surface-specific invariant logic must already be committed. Otherwise a uniformly broken response could teach the validator that broken output is normal.

5. **An identically failing soak must not pass.** Drift-only would permit that, which is the decisive defect in A. Uniform quality failure trips the absolute quality gate. Uniformly pathological latency trips the 10-second p95 or 60-second hard ceiling. A run that is merely slower than desired but remains under those ceilings can pass; that is an explicit consequence of having no external latency baseline.

6. **Shared-host noise receives a precommitted rerun rule, never discretionary forgiveness.** Run a fixed CPU/process/filesystem reference probe adjacent to every invocation and record host CPU time attributable outside the harness process tree. A run is mechanically marked contaminated only if both conditions hold:

   - Reference-probe late/early p50 ratio exceeds 1.25.
   - At least 20% of late-window intervals show external processes consuming at least 80% of available CPU capacity.

   Contamination is calculated before considering whether the agent passed or failed and forces exactly one complete rerun. It never converts red to green, never excuses quality or resource-slope failures, and cannot be asserted manually. A valid red fails immediately; a contaminated first run requires the rerun; a contaminated rerun fails certification. This prevents host noise from manufacturing a final red while denying the certifier an unlimited “noisy machine” escape hatch.
