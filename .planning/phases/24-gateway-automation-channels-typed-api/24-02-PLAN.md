<objective>
Turn scheduling from a session-local background loop into a persistent automation plane with exactly one owner, the full trigger vocabulary, and bounded history, retry and continuation — and prove that killing the runtime mid-fire loses nothing and duplicates nothing.

Purpose: Phase 22's fourth Success Criterion deliberately deferred persistent scheduling to this phase. The scheduling crate already parses expressions, persists jobs, ticks, and ring-buffers a fire history — but it is spawned per session at engine boot and can also be spawned as a detached background process against the same store, so ownership is assumed rather than held. Phase 24's second Success Criterion needs scheduled, event-driven, webhook, polling and commitment work with bounded history, retry, continuation and delivery; that needs one owner and one delivery spine.
Output: A leased single-owner schedule hosted by the gateway from 24-01, the full trigger vocabulary with a bound on each type, bounded retry and cross-restart continuation routed through the exactly-once delivery ledger, natural-language authoring that produces a reviewable expression, and live evidence that a hard kill mid-fire yields exactly one completion after restart.
</objective>
