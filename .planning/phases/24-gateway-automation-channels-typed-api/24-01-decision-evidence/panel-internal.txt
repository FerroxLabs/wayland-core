INTERNAL ADVERSARIAL PASS — F24-01 Task 3
Written to argue AGAINST the position codex, gemini and kimi converged on
(win-scheduled-task), not to ratify it. Author: the executing agent.

The three external members agreed within minutes and for the same reason:
the binding rule knocked out win-service-scm, and among what was left only
schtasks had a registration model. That is a process of elimination
dressed as a decision, and elimination arguments are exactly where a panel
is weakest. Four objections, strongest first.

OBJECTION 1 — THE CONSENSUS'S OWN EVIDENCE DID NOT MEASURE THE PROPERTY IT
CLAIMED. This is the serious one.

The `schtasks` probe registered a task, ran it, slept twelve seconds INSIDE
its own ssh session, and read 119 heartbeats there. Every one of those
beats was written while the spawning session was still alive. What that
measures is "the task runs". The property the option needs is "the process
outlives the session that started it" — and the probe never left the
session, so it never tested that. The transcript's only evidence for
survival-past-session-end was a stray child found alive forty seconds after
its session had ended, which was discovered by accident while cleaning up.
Accidental evidence is not evidence: nobody designed the conditions, nobody
controlled the variables, and it was noticed rather than measured.

All three external members read that transcript and called it proof of a
"session-independent parent". None of them noticed the observation window
sat inside the session. A panel that misreads the same artifact three ways
in the same direction is not three votes; it is one vote counted three
times.

DISPOSITION: this objection was ACCEPTED and then CLOSED BY MEASUREMENT
rather than by argument. A fifth probe was run: register, run, delete the
task registration, exit so the ssh session ends, and observe from a LATER,
SEPARATE session — the identical shape the two detach probes use.
Result (`measure-schtasks-survival.txt`): beats advanced 49 → 89 across the
two reads, one live process, with the task registration already deleted.
The property is now measured on purpose. The objection stands as a
criticism of how the consensus was reached; it no longer stands as a
criticism of the conclusion.

OBJECTION 2 — "INTERACTIVE ONLY" IS NOT A SERVICE, AND THE PROBE SHOWED IT.
The task the probe actually registered came back `Logon Mode: Interactive
only`, `Run As User: seand`. On a headless or unattended box that runtime
does not start at all. Criterion 1 says NATIVE SERVICE LIFECYCLE. A
mechanism that requires a human to be logged in is closer to
win-detached-only than the consensus admits — it differs mainly in who
types the start command, not in whether the platform owns the process.

The counter, and it is a real one: the two families this must stay
symmetric with are BOTH per-user. The repository ships a per-user launchd
AGENT and a per-user systemd USER unit, not a system daemon and not a
system service. Both of those also do not start before login. Choosing SCM
would make Windows the ONLY family that runs pre-login and the only one
that demands elevation — that is asymmetry introduced in the name of
symmetry. The honest statement is that "starts before login" is a property
NO family has today, and the Windows option that preserves that shape is
the scheduled task. This objection therefore reduces to a documentation
requirement, not a mechanism defect, and it is recorded as an accepted cost.

OBJECTION 3 — SCM WAS DISQUALIFIED BY A PROBE ARTIFACT, NOT BY A PROPERTY
OF THE MECHANISM. The 1053 means a sixty-line console executable did not
answer the service control handshake. Of course it did not; it was never
written to. That is a fact about the probe, not about whether wayland-core
could implement the handshake — and letting probe design pick the
architecture is a category error. The binding rule was supposed to stop the
panel arguing its way to an unavailable mechanism; here it may have stopped
it choosing an available one that simply was not built yet.

This is the objection I find hardest to dismiss, and I do not dismiss it. I
narrow it. The rule's purpose is to stop a phase committing to a property
it has not seen. Whether wayland-core CAN become a Windows service is not
in doubt; whether it does so inside THIS plan's declared files, with the
drain budget reconciled against the service stop timeout, is a substantial
piece of unbuilt work — and the option's own cons say so. Choosing SCM here
would mean the phase's Windows lifecycle rests on something not written,
not measured, and not budgeted. Choosing the scheduled task means it rests
on something measured twice. The rule reaches the right answer even though
the reasoning the three members gave for it was too quick.

OBJECTION 4 — RESTART-ON-FAILURE IS NOT SERVICE RECOVERY, AND CRITERION 5
ASKS FOR RECOVERY. Criterion 5 requires the platform's OWN mechanism to
bring the runtime back after a hard kill that allows no drain. Task
Scheduler's restart-on-failure is capped in count, delayed in time, and
keyed off the action's exit — it is genuinely weaker than an SCM recovery
policy. The consensus asserted equivalence; it is not equivalent.

DISPOSITION: this is a REAL residual risk and it is NOT closed by anything
measured here. It is recorded as an open risk carried into 24-04, whose
journey must actually kill the gateway and confirm the scheduler — not the
journey script — brings it back. If it does not, Criterion 5 does not close
on Windows and that must be reported rather than engineered around. I am
recording this rather than letting the decision absorb it.

CONCLUSION OF THE ADVERSARIAL PASS
I set out to argue against win-scheduled-task and could not sustain it on
the merits. Objection 1 was real and was closed by running the probe that
should have been run first. Objection 2 reduces to a documented cost that
the Unix families already pay. Objection 3 is a fair criticism of the
panel's reasoning that nonetheless lands on the same answer. Objection 4 is
a genuine unclosed risk that belongs in the record and in 24-04's scope, not
in the mechanism choice.

The one thing I will not concede to the consensus is its confidence. Three
members read a transcript that did not show what they said it showed. The
conclusion survived only because the missing measurement was taken
afterwards.
