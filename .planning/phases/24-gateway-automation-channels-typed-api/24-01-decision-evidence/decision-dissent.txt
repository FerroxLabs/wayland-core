DISSENT — F24-01 Task 3. The losing arguments in their own terms.

The panel was unanimous, so no member dissented. That makes recording the
losing arguments MORE important, not less: a unanimous panel is the one
most likely to have agreed too fast, and the record below is what a later
reader needs in order to reopen this if the risk in it lands.

ARGUMENT FOR win-service-scm, in its own terms, and it is the strongest
option on paper. It is the platform's own answer to a persistent background
runtime. It starts before login, survives console close BY CONSTRUCTION
rather than by flag hygiene, restarts under the platform's own recovery
policy, and is inspectable with the tools a Windows operator already uses
every day — services.msc, `sc query`, the event log. It is the closest
structural match to a launch agent and a systemd unit, so the three families
would stay one contract with one implementation each rather than two
contracts and a special case.

Its defeat here was NOT a judgement that it is the wrong mechanism. It was
disqualified because a sixty-line console probe returned 1053 — a fact about
the probe, not about the mechanism. That is a category error worth naming
plainly: probe design should not pick architecture. The counter-argument
that carried, and it is narrower than the vote made it look, is only this:
the option's central property was never observed, and making it observable
means writing a service-control handshake and reconciling the drain budget
against the service stop timeout — substantial work the plan does not budget
and the option's own cons already name. If someone later builds a
handshake-capable binary, this decision deserves to be revisited on the
merits rather than treated as settled.

ARGUMENT FOR win-detached-only, in its own terms. It is the smallest change
that fixes something genuinely broken. The measurement proved the current
spawn is a real defect, and correcting the creation flags stands on its own
regardless of which registration model is chosen later — it is the only part
of this decision that is certainly right. Committing to a registration model
now, on one afternoon of probing, risks locking the phase to schtasks when
the evidence really only supports "the flags fix works".

Its defeat: Criterion 1 says NATIVE SERVICE LIFECYCLE, and Criterion 5 needs
the PLATFORM to bring the runtime back after a hard kill. A process an
operator restarts by hand at every logon is neither.

ARGUMENT FOR win-defer, in its own terms. Nothing is built on an
unauthorized mechanism and the gap stays visible in the record instead of
being absorbed into an implementation detail. Given how thin the evidence
under schtasks was BEFORE the fifth probe was run, deferral would have been
the defensible answer at the moment the three external members voted.

Its defeat: by the time the decision was written, two mechanisms had
measured usable. Deferring with usable evidence in hand records a gap that
does not exist.

THE OBJECTION THAT NEARLY CHANGED THE OUTCOME, recorded because it says
something about the panel rather than about the options. The `schtasks`
transcript the three external members were given did NOT measure survival
past session end — its twelve-second observation window sat inside the
spawning session. All three read it as proof of a "session-independent
parent". A panel that misreads one artifact three ways in the same direction
is one vote counted three times, and the conclusion survived only because
the missing probe was run afterwards. Anyone re-reading this decision should
weight the four captures by what each MEASURED, not by the tally.
