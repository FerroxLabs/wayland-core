<objective>
Give Fleet work a durable task ledger — dependencies, attempts, claims, liveness, ownership, handoffs, completion and parent wake-up — that survives a real process kill and a real restart on both Linux and Windows, without executing anything twice and without losing a completion.

Purpose: This is Success Criterion 2. Today's fanout has a dispatcher, a shard timeout and a blackboard; none of that knows, after a crash, which tasks were claimed, by whom, how many times they have been attempted, or whether a finished task's result ever reached the parent. The hard part is not the data model — it is that a claim held by a dead process is indistinguishable from a claim held by a slow one, so revocation without fencing produces exactly the duplicate execution the criterion forbids, rarely enough to look correct.
Output: A measured durability baseline of today's fanout; an authorized claim-revocation and fencing model with its costs stated on both platforms; the ledger built as Task records in the same chain as the Goal, above the existing executors rather than replacing them; and a live exercise that kills the real product process mid-fanout, restarts it, and proves from the real journal that nothing ran twice and nothing was lost.
</objective>
