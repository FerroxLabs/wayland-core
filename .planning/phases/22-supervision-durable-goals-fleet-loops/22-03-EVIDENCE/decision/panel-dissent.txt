DISSENT RECORD — every losing option's strongest argument, and why it did not carry.
Preserved because a 4-0 panel is exactly the result most likely to have flattened
a real objection, and the objections here are not weak.

--- os-process-liveness ---
STRONGEST ARGUMENT FOR IT: its only soundness objection died under measurement.
The con said "process-identifier reuse makes a naive probe unsafe and it needs a
start-time or handle binding to be sound". Measurement 2 shows that binding is
AVAILABLE ON BOTH HOSTS - /proc/<pid>/stat field 22 on Linux, Win32 creation time
on Windows, each stable across repeated reads and distinct per process. Combined
with the Linux baseline (8 bwrap containers to 0, 17 worker shells to 0, 0 DONE
lines after waiting past the full sleep), an OS probe on a single-host fleet is a
genuine liveness verdict rather than an inference, at a fraction of the fencing
surface. For the fleet this phase actually builds - one host - it is arguably
sufficient.
WHY IT DID NOT CARRY: it answers only the revocation half. Its own OPTION line
records refuses_late_write=nothing. A wedged-but-alive owner that recovers is
indistinguishable from the current owner, so its duplicate-EFFECT window is
unbounded no matter how good the liveness signal is. Both external members and
the adversarial pass reached this independently. The "single host is enough"
premise also expires the moment reassignment crosses hosts, which the option's
own con names.

--- heartbeat-liveness ---
STRONGEST ARGUMENT FOR IT: it is the only option that detects a WEDGED owner - one
that is alive, holding a claim, and making no progress. A lease detects that too
but only after its full term; an OS probe never detects it at all, because the
process is genuinely alive. It is also the cheapest to observe and maps onto the
worker status surface that already exists (heartbeat.rs:56).
WHY IT DID NOT CARRY: Measurement 3 turned its stated con into a number. A process
stopped for 12 seconds missed 12 of 12 beats, so any missed-beat threshold below
12 declares a healthy-but-suspended fleet dead and reassigns all of it at once.
And like os-process-liveness it has no fencing half. It survives in the committed
design as an OPTIONAL ACCELERATOR - codex's phrase - that can shorten revocation
latency, never as the thing that makes a late write refusable.

--- escalate ---
STRONGEST ARGUMENT FOR IT, and it is the one that nearly carried: the measured
baseline says Success Criterion 2 cannot be closed in this tree at all. A killed
fanout CANNOT BE RESTARTED - orphaned reservations exhaust the aggregate budget,
nothing calls cleanup_all() at dispatch, and a human must delete
.swarm-worktrees/ by hand (F-2, HIGH, open). Workers fail as a function of
elapsed run time, 4/4 at 1s but 1/4 at 10s, while real fanout workers run for
minutes (F-3, HIGH, open, not root-caused). Any claim model layered on that
executor states windows nobody can honor. Choosing a mechanism now risks
manufacturing exactly the false confidence this program has already paid for
once.
WHY IT DID NOT CARRY: `escalate` is defined in this plan as "the criterion cannot
be met WITHOUT A MECHANISM OUTSIDE THIS PLAN'S SCOPE". That is not what the
evidence shows. It shows two open defects INSIDE scope, already owned by another
agent. Escalating would be using the wrong instrument to report a true
observation. The correct handling - and what this lane does - is to commit the
mechanism on the evidence, and separately and loudly report that the criterion
remains OPEN behind F-2 and F-3. The decision and the criterion are recorded
separately rather than one being used to launder the other.

--- ARGUMENTS MADE AGAINST THE COMMITTED OPTION (lease-with-fencing-token) ---
These are real and are NOT dismissed; they are converted into shipping conditions.

1. It defends a hazard the only measured platform says does not occur. The Linux
   kill reaps the whole tree, so there is no superseded owner alive to fence.
   ANSWER: the Windows kill leg was never validly run, and reasoning
   "descendants always die" from one platform is precisely the error that
   produced F-1, where a race Windows happened to win was read as a race that
   was absent. An ordering-based refusal is the option that does not depend on
   the unmeasured platform's process semantics being what we assumed.

2. A PARTIALLY fenced system is worse than an honestly unfenced one, because it
   creates the belief that duplicate effects are impossible while two real paths
   still admit them. Two of six enumerated paths are review-only today, and the
   enumeration is explicitly a lower bound, not exhaustive.
   ANSWER: accepted as the binding condition on the commitment rather than
   waved away. All four members raised it independently. It is recorded as
   CONDITION 1 below and the criterion is not claimed met until it holds.

3. The stated dup_window_ms=30000 is a design parameter, not a measurement.
   ANSWER: true, and labelled as such in the bundle the panel judged. What IS
   measured is that the duplicate-EFFECT bound is 0 for this option and
   unbounded for the two revocation-only ones; that comparison, not the lease
   term, is what decided it.

--- CONDITIONS ATTACHED TO THE COMMITMENT ---
CONDITION 1 (from codex, corroborated by kimi and the adversarial pass): ship only
after every AUTHORITATIVE effect path is routed through a structurally guarded
commit API that requires the claim epoch and compares it atomically against the
ledger's committed epoch. Status and diagnostic writes may be exempt ONLY if they
cannot affect task completion, reassignment, accounting, or dependency release.
The workspace reservation accounting path (worktree_manager.rs:235) does affect
accounting and is therefore NOT exempt.
CONDITION 2: the Windows kill leg must be validly run before Success Criterion 2
is claimed on both platforms. It is currently unmeasured, and the committed
option was chosen partly BECAUSE that is unmeasured.
