SUSPEND BEHAVIOR — what does a stopped process observe when it resumes?
Measured live on hetzner-dsm by SIGSTOPping a real process for 12s with a
notional 1s beat interval, then SIGCONTing it.

stopped_s=12 observed_advance_s=12 missed_beats=12 catchup_beats=12
SUSPEND-CLOCK: stopped_s=12 observed_advance_s=12 missed_beats=12 catchup_beats=12

WHAT THIS SETTLES, CONCRETELY:
1. The monotonic clock advances THROUGH an OS-level stop. It is not paused with
   the process. So a TIME-BASED lease held by a stopped worker expires on
   schedule even though the worker did nothing wrong, and a suspended machine
   expires every lease at once.
2. A heartbeat scheme misses EVERY beat across the stop - 12 of 12 here. With
   any missed-beat threshold below 12 the whole fleet is declared dead and
   reassigned simultaneously. That is the heartbeat option's mass-reassignment
   con, measured rather than argued.
3. Neither effect touches an ORDERING-based refusal. A monotonic fencing token
   compares "is this token older than the one the ledger has committed", which
   is unaffected by how much wall time passed while the owner was stopped. The
   lease-with-fencing-token pro that it "degrades correctly when a machine is
   suspended, because the refusal is ordering-based rather than time-based" is
   therefore supported by this measurement.

CAVEAT STATED HONESTLY: SIGSTOP/SIGCONT models a suspended or descheduled
process. It is not a full machine suspend-to-RAM, where the wall clock jumps but
the monotonic clock's behavior is platform-specific. The direction of the result
is the same and the mass-reassignment conclusion holds for both, but the exact
observed_advance for a real S3 suspend was not measured.
