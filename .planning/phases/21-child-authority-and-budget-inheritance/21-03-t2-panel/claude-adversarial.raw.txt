1. THE STRONGEST FORM OF THE ARGUMENT THE OTHER THREE DID NOT MAKE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The plan's own framing says the expensive failure mode is "shipping a real
behaviour change to fix an imaginary defect", and that DISPROVE is the cheapest
correct outcome WHEN IT IS THE TRUE ONE. Nobody on this panel tested whether it
is the true one. All three members skipped straight from "the finding is
confirmed at the seam" to "therefore repair it". Those are different claims.

Take F21-02-02, the one all three call the easy, contained, obviously-correct
win. What is the actual defect? `with_requested_approvals` returns
`Self::smart(Bypass, Child)` when handed `(Bypass, PolicySource::Child)`. Now
ask the DISPROVE question literally, in the plan's own words: did the case
construct "a request no surface can express"?

Yes. It did exactly that, and the corpus SAYS SO IN ITS OWN WORDS. The corpus
records the approval dimension as NO-CHANNEL, not ALLOWED, on all eight
combinations across both platforms. `PolicySource::Child` has no production
constructor. The blast-radius probe just confirmed it a third way: ratcheting
that branch changed ZERO functional tests, which is only possible because
nothing in the product ever reaches it. Three independent measurements agree
that this input cannot be constructed by any surface.

So F21-02-02 is, on the plan's own definition, textbook DISPROVE: a case that
asked a question production cannot be asked. The corrected case is the
NO-CHANNEL canary — which already exists and already passes. The finding's real
content is not "approval can be widened"; it is "if someone later builds a child
approval channel, they must build a ratchet with it". That is a REQUIREMENT ON
FUTURE WORK, and it is already enforced by a structural canary that fails the day
any file outside `wcore-types/src/execution_policy.rs` names `PolicySource::Child`.

And the cost of "repairing" it is not zero, which is the part the other three
waved through. It moves `source_inputs_digest`, which moves the `ready`
descriptor the shipped binary emits, which the linked Desktop checkpoint records
as a hard renegotiation error, in a repository whose consumer half cannot be
reached from this checkout. So the proposal is: ship a wire-provenance bump that
a Desktop lane must absorb, in exchange for changing the behaviour of a code path
that three separate measurements say nothing can reach. That is the imaginary
defect the plan warned about, wearing the costume of a one-line fix.

2. AND THE SAME MOVE, WEAKER BUT REAL, ON F21-02-01
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The census recorded three mitigations bounding HIGH-1. The corpus's tool case
tripped over the second of them — anything beyond the read-only floor forces
`IsolatedMutation` and durable workspace preparation refuses. The other three
members treat that as "the corpus failed to reach the seam". Read it the other
way: the product refused, at a real surface, in a real hermetic workspace, on
both platforms, in every combination, and the child got no Bash. Zero widenings
in 88 executions. The invariant Success Criterion 1 states — the child must not
obtain what the parent does not hold — HELD. What did not hold is a claim about
the MECHANISM by which it held, and Phase 21's criterion is about the property,
not about which layer delivers it.

That is not a full DISPROVE and I will not overclaim it as one. But it is enough
to say the fixture's parent held a configuration a real read-only parent would
not: a read-only parent that can nonetheless authorize an isolated-mutation
worktree is a contradiction the case built into itself.

3. WHERE MY OWN ARGUMENT BREAKS, STATED BECAUSE HIDING IT WOULD BE WORSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Three objections defeat the case above, and I record them rather than let this
member's position stand on a suppressed rebuttal.

First, and decisively: PHASE 21 EXISTS BECAUSE OF EXACTLY THIS ARGUMENT. The
phase's own framing states that F21-02 currently holds VACUOUSLY — the property
is satisfied by the absence of a request channel, not by enforcement — and that
this is the defect, not the defence. "Nothing can reach it today" is the premise
the phase was created to reject. Using it as the reason not to repair inverts the
phase's purpose. Against F21-02-02 my argument therefore proves too much: it
would forbid ever converting a vacuous property into an enforced one.

Second, procedural and dispositive: the plan permits DISPROVE only where the
triage proposed DISPROVE, and the triage proposed FIX for all three. Authorizing
a corpus edit over a finding triaged FIX is the exact forgery the RED-over-GREEN
rule names. `disprove-and-correct` is therefore unavailable to this panel as a
matter of rule, not just of judgement — and a member arguing for an unavailable
option is arguing to break the gate, not to win it.

Third, on F21-02-01 specifically: the codebase's own existing unit test at
spawner.rs:4357 grants Bash to a child under a parent that was never consulted.
That is not a corpus artifact; it is the product's own test asserting the
widening as intended behaviour. You cannot disprove a finding the code proves.

4. THE ONE PLACE I THINK THE PANEL IS ACTUALLY WRONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two of the three members (gemini-pro, kimi-k3) voted to authorize F21-02-01's
repair. They should not have, and codex-sol is right against them: the proposed
change wires `parent_tool_authority` at bootstrap.rs and NOWHERE ELSE, while
production constructs `AgentSpawner` at several further sites —
`engine.rs:12061`, `orchestration/anvil/seat.rs:91`, `wcore-cli/src/workflow.rs:173`
and `wcore-cli/src/crucible.rs:36` among them, the last two with no `#[cfg(test)]`
anywhere above them. At every one of those the field stays `None` and the
intersection is skipped. The fix as designed closes one of five doors and
advertises the corridor as secured. That is the same fail-open shape as the
PolicyGate defect this very plan is declining, reintroduced one finding later —
and worse, three of those four sites construct their spawner from a `Config`
with no parent `ToolRegistry` in scope at all, so there is nothing there to
intersect against without introducing the parent-tool-authority concept as a
first-class thing across the spawner API. That is an architecture change, not a
wiring line, and it does not fit a two-iteration cap.

So my substantive position on the per-finding split is codex-sol's, not
gemini-pro's or kimi-k3's. But my SET-LEVEL vote is not `authorize-partial`,
because I have been asked to test the consensus rather than join it, and because
the strongest untested case here — that the system was right and the corpus was

somebody actually held rather than as a paragraph in a losing rationale.

I reject `authorize-full`: 22 functional tests break, and the useful version of

I reject `decline-all`: F21-02-02 is a genuine cheap conversion of vacuous-true
into enforced-true even if I have just argued it is unnecessary, and throwing it
away buys nothing measurable.
