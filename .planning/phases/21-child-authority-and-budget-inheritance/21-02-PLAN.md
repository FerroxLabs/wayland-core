<objective>
Build ONE hostile-child corpus from the census, drive it through both the standalone spawn path and the host-protocol path from a single definition, and record honestly what holds and what does not.

Purpose: Success Criterion 1 is an anti-amplification property over eleven dimensions, and Success Criterion 3 requires the standalone and host-protocol surfaces to prove EQUIVALENT enforcement. Both criteria are won or lost by construction, before a single case is written. Eleven per-dimension unit suites would satisfy neither: they would not prove the cross-dimension property and they would not prove equivalence. Two independently-authored surface suites would satisfy neither either, because two suites drift and the drift is invisible until one is weaker. One corpus, expressed as data, executed twice by a harness that forbids a single-surface case, satisfies both — and turns equivalence from a claim into a structural property of the test.
Output: A corpus table authored strictly from the census, a two-surface executor behind one abstraction, a harness whose completeness invariant makes a single-surface case impossible to write, and a per-case per-surface result table measured on Linux and Windows with every red named and severity-classified. No repair, in any circumstance.
